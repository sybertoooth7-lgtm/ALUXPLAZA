# Threat Intelligence Briefing — Service Methodology

_A real, deliverable research/reporting service. Replaces "Federated Intelligence" fiction with an honest aggregation-and-summary product grounded in named, current sources._

## What this service actually is

A periodic (e.g. quarterly) written briefing that aggregates and summarizes current, real threat trends relevant to a specific client's industry — sourced from actual vendor threat reports, government advisories, and security research, not a fictional "federated AI network." Every claim in the briefing traces back to a named, dated, real source.

## Who it's for

E-commerce and small web-business operators (a natural first client base: Shopify merchants like VersaPro) who don't have time to track security research themselves but want to know what's actually changing in the threat landscape that affects their specific business type.

## Scope of a typical engagement

| Phase        | What happens                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Deliverable               |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| 1. Scoping   | Confirm the client's industry/platform (e-commerce/Shopify, SaaS, etc.) and what they most care about (fraud losses? data breaches? uptime?)                                                                                         | Scoping note              |
| 2. Research  | Pull current material from vendor threat reports (e.g. Verizon DBIR, Kaspersky, MRC, Recorded Future), government advisories (CISA), and reputable security news, filtered to what's relevant to the client's specific business type | Raw research notes        |
| 3. Synthesis | Distill into a short, plain-English briefing: what's trending, why it matters for this specific business, and what to actually do about it                                                                                           | Draft briefing            |
| 4. Delivery  | Reviewed, finalized briefing document, plus a short verbal/written walkthrough of the top 2-3 items the client should act on                                                                                                         | Final briefing (PDF/Word) |
| 5. Cadence   | Repeat on an agreed schedule (quarterly is typical — threat landscape reports themselves are mostly published annually/quarterly, so more frequent than that adds little signal)                                                     | Recurring briefing        |

## What's in the actual briefing (see `threat-intelligence-briefing-sample.docx`)

- **Executive summary** — 3-5 sentence plain-English take on what matters this period
- **Trend sections** — each one names the real source and date, explains the trend, and gives a concrete "what this means for you" translation for the client's specific business
- **Recommended actions** — tied back to other real services where relevant (e.g. a skimming trend point ties to the Access Control Audit; a phishing trend ties to staff awareness)
- **Sources** — every source named and dated, so the client (or anyone) can verify independently

## What requires your judgment (the actual service, beyond aggregation)

- Filtering signal from noise: most threat reports are written for enterprise security teams, not a solo Shopify merchant — the real value is translating "48% of breaches involved ransomware" into "here's the 2 things that matters for a store your size"
- Knowing which sources are credible (established vendors, government advisories, peer-reviewed research) versus SEO content farms repackaging old news
- Connecting trends to the client's actual, specific setup — not just repeating a generic industry statistic

## Framing this honestly to clients

- This is a **briefing, not a live feed** — it reflects the best available research as of the writing date, not real-time "federated intelligence."
- Don't inflate specificity — if a source says "48% of breaches involved ransomware" industry-wide, don't imply that number applies specifically to the client's business.
- Recommend clients treat this as context for prioritization, not a replacement for their own audits/monitoring (Access Control Audit, Honeypot Monitoring) which produce evidence about their specific systems.

## Suggested starting price point

- A flat fee per briefing, or a lower recurring quarterly retainer if the client wants ongoing coverage — pricing should reflect research time (typically a few hours per briefing for a well-scoped industry), not a "live AI monitoring" premium.
